## 1. The realizer class and the modulus convention

Accelerated Syracuse map on odd `x`: `T(x) = (3x+1)/2^{v(x)}`, `v(x) = v₂(3x+1)`.
A valuation word is `w = (v₁,…,v_n)` with `v_k ≥ 1`; `S_k = v₁+⋯+v_k`, `S = S_n`,
`|w| = n`. The affine identity is `2^{S_k}x_k = 3^k x + C_k` with append
recurrence `C' = 3C + 2^S`, equivalently `C_w = Σ_{k=1}^{n} 3^{n−k}2^{S_{k−1}}`.

> **Proposition 1.** An odd `m` realizes `w` with exact valuations iff
> `3^n m + C_w ≡ 2^S (mod 2^{S+1})`. The realizer set is exactly one residue
> class mod `2^{S+1}`, and `m(w)` denotes its least positive member.

*Proof.* Two steps. (a) The top divisibility `2^{S_n} | 3^n m + C_n` implies
every earlier one: from `C_n = 3C_{n−1} + 2^{S_{n−1}}` we get
`3^n m + C_n = 3(3^{n−1}m + C_{n−1}) + 2^{S_{n−1}}`, and `2^{S_{n−1}}` divides
both the left side and the trailing term, so it divides `3(3^{n−1}m+C_{n−1})`
and hence `3^{n−1}m+C_{n−1}`. The same computation gives `2^{v_n} | 3x_{n−1}+1`,
so each `x_k` with `k<n` is automatically odd. (b) The one remaining condition
is that `x_n` be odd, worth exactly one further bit; it is what upgrades the
integrality modulus `2^S` to `2^{S+1}`. ∎

**Convention note, stated once.** The *integrality* class alone is mod `2^S`;
the *exact-valuation* class is mod `2^{S+1}`. The distinction is not cosmetic —
it is the difference between a realizer and the least realizer, and it is the
source of the §2 reconciliation below.

**Verification.** An independent implementation (`src_indep/`, sharing no code
with `src/`) builds the class by backward recursion,
`x ≡ 3^{-1}(2^{v₁}a' − 1) (mod 2^{v₁+M'})` from the base "the empty word is
realized by every odd `x`". The modulus `2^{S+1}` emerges with no input, and a
**full scan of every odd `m < 2^{S+1}`** on 243 words confirms the class is a
singleton below `2^{S+1}` with `M = S+1` in every case: **0 failures**.

## 2. One defect: the two-run formula, and a notation reconciliation

For a run of ones the conjugation `y = x+1` linearises the map: `v_k = 1` gives
`x_k+1 = 3(x_{k−1}+1)/2`, so `x_k+1 = (3/2)^k(x_0+1)`. Hence a leading run of
`u` ones forces `2^u | m+1`, and a following letter `q ≥ 2` forces
`2^{u+1} ‖ m+1`. Writing `m+1 = 2^{u+1}A` with `A` odd and `n = q+v`:

> **Proposition 2.** Let `m+1 = 2^{u+1}A`, `A` odd, `n = q+v`, and
> `y = (3^{u+1}A−1)/2^{q−1}` the odd orbit value after `1^u q`. The run of `v`
> ones is governed by `v₂(y+1)`, and the two conditions are **distinct**:
>
> | orbit condition | meaning | congruence |
> |---|---|---|
> | `v₂(y+1) ≥ v+1` | realizes the finite word `1^u q 1^v` | `3^{u+1}A ≡ 1−2^{q−1} (mod 2^n)` |
> | `v₂(y+1) = v+1` | …and the next valuation is ≥ 2 | `3^{u+1}A ≡ 1−2^{q−1}+2^n (mod 2^{n+1})` |
>
> Write `m(P)` and `m_closed(P)` for the least realizer under each.

*Proof.* After `1^u` the orbit is at `2·3^u A − 1`, so `3x+1 = 2(3^{u+1}A−1)`
and `v₂ = q` forces `v₂(3^{u+1}A−1) = q−1`, giving `x_{u+1} = y`. A run of `v`
ones needs `2^v | y+1`; the final value `3^v(y+1)/2^v − 1` is odd exactly when
`2^{v+1} | y+1`. For the next valuation,
`3x_n+1 = 2(3^{v+1}(y+1)/2^{v+1} − 1)`, divisible by 4 precisely when
`2^{v+1} ‖ y+1`. ∎

**The source's finer modulus is not an error.** It is the *closed episode*
condition — the end of a whole episode rather than an arbitrary finite prefix —
and is equally legitimate. The earlier "typo vs convention" dilemma dissolves:
both rows are used below.

**The two realizers can differ by many bits.** `m_closed(P) ∈ {m(P),
m(P)+2^{S_P+1}}`, so they differ by one *modulus* — but their *heights* can
differ by far more than one bit, since `m(P)` may sit well below `2^{S_P+1}`.
At `u=11, q=5, v=4`: `m(P) = 4095`, `m_closed(P) = 2101247 = 4095+2^21`, so
`log₂(m_closed/m) = 9.0032`. The measured supremum grows with the grid (7.4237
at `u,v ≤ 10`; 9.0032 at ≤14; 9.4294 at ≤18), so it is **not** an `O(1)`
correction. **An earlier claim here that the gap is at most one bit is
withdrawn**: one extra congruence bit is not one extra bit of height.

**Placement reduction.** Minimality of `A` is governed by `3^{u+1}A ≡ c`, i.e.
by where `c·3^{−(u+1)}` falls in `(Z/2^{n})`; dually, by the ternary digits of
`2^{−n}` modulo `3^{u+1}`. This is the one-defect placement problem, and it is
the one-dimensional case of the obstruction in §8.

## 3. Two defects: the exact closed form and the denominator lemma

Let `w = 1^u q_a 1^v q_b 1^t`, `n = u+v+t+2`, `S = u+v+t+q_a+q_b`.

> **Theorem 3 (R1).**
> `C_w = 3^n − 3^{v+t+1}2^{u+1} + 3^{v+t+1}2^{u+q_a} − 3^t 2^{u+q_a+v+1} + 3^t 2^{u+q_a+v+q_b} − 2^S.`

Equivalently, from Proposition 1,
`m(w) ≡ −1 − 3^{−(u+1)}2^{u+1}(2^{q_a−1}−1) − 3^{−(u+v+2)}2^{u+q_a+v+1}(2^{q_b−1}−1) (mod 2^{S+1})`,
which exhibits `2^{u+1} ‖ m+1` since both bracketed cofactors are odd.
Degenerate check: at `q_a = q_b = 1` the formula collapses to `3^n − 2^n`, the
all-ones value. **Verified: 0 mismatches / 19773** against the append
recurrence.

> **Lemma 4 (R2, corrected form).** The anchor is `3^{−n}(2^S − C_w)`, so its
> reduced denominator is `D = 3^{n − v₃(2^S − C_w)}`. On the **interior**
> stratum `u, v, t ≥ 1` we have `v₃(2^S − C_w) = 0`, hence `D = 3^n`: the
> anchor is 3-coprime and no denominator-reducing bridge exists there.

*Proof sketch.* With `t ≥ 1` every monomial of `C_w` except `−2^S` carries a
factor 3, so `C_w ≡ −2^S (mod 3)` and `2^S − C_w ≡ 2^{S+1} ≢ 0 (mod 3)`. ∎

**The boundary strata are real and must be carved out.** The premise
`gcd(C_w, 3^n) = 1` holds on all 19773 configurations, but it does **not**
imply `D = 3^n`, because `D` is governed by `gcd(2^S − C_w, 3^n)`. Stratified
over `|w| ≤ 44`:

| `u≥1` | `v≥1` | `t≥1` | configs | `v₃(2^S−C_w)` histogram |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T | T | T | 19773 | `0:19773` |
| T | T | F | 1521 | `0:507 1:507 2:507` |
| T | F | T | 1521 | `0:1521` |
| T | F | F | 117 | `0:39 1:39 2:26 3:12 4:1` |
| F | T | T | 1521 | `0:1521` |
| F | T | F | 117 | `0:39 1:39 2:39` |
| F | F | T | 117 | `0:117` |
| F | F | F | 9 | `0:3 1:3 2:3` |

Reductions (`v₃>0`) occur on **1176** configurations with `t=0` and on **0**
with `t ≥ 1`: the reduction is confined to `t=0` exactly. The extreme witness
is the `x=1` cycle word `w=(2,2)`, where `m(w)=1`, `2^S−C_w=9`, `v₃=2` and
`D=1` — the degenerate stratum is precisely the fixed point. Accordingly
**this note states the family as `u,v,t ≥ 1`** and treats `t=0` separately.

**The denominator is representative-dependent.** Both one-sided lifts pin one
representative each; the canonical Lemma-H datum is the *balanced* lift
`N* ≡ −C (mod 2^S)` with `|N*| ≤ 2^{S−1}`, giving `D = 3^{n−v₃(N*)}`. On the
family `u,v,t ∈ 1..12` (15552 configurations), verified independently:

- both one-sided representatives (`−C` and `2^S−C`) are 3-coprime
  **everywhere**: 0/15552 each;
- the balanced lift reduces on **5263/15552 = 0.3384**, with `v₃(N*)`
  distribution `{0:10289, 1:3478, 2:1199, 3:417, 4:123, 5:32, 6:9, 7:3, 8:1,
  9:1}` against the geometric(2/3) prediction `{10368, 3456, 1152, 384, 128,
  42.7, 14.2, 4.7, 1.6, 0.5}` — generic 3-adic behaviour, max `v₃ = 9`;
- dangerous-window probe: median `log₂m/(S+1)` is **0.9615** at `v₃(N*)=0`
  versus **0.9620** at `v₃(N*)>0`, a difference of 0.00046.

So denominator reduction is **generic noise carrying no signal about `m`**, not
a lever. Genuine *structural* reduction occurs only on the `t=0` boundary,
deepest at the all-defect corner, whose witness `2^k` is the `x=1`
fixed-point word — reduced denominators mark cycle proximity, consistent with
the cycle-denominator identification (Lagarias 1990). "No denominator-reducing
bridge" should be stated that way, with `t=0` and `v=0` carved out.

## 4. The suffix-combination floor (per-configuration certificate)

Let `P = 1^u q_a 1^v`, `P' = 1^u q_a`, `Q = 1^v q_b 1^t`.

> **Theorem 5.** (a) `m(w) ≥ m(P)`; (b)
> `m(w) ≥ ⌈(2^{S_{P'}}m(Q) − C_{P'})/3^{u+1}⌉`; (c)
> `m(w) ≥ m_closed(P) ≥ m(P)`. Hence `F_c' := max{(b),(c)}` is an
> unconditional closed-form floor, and `F_c' ≥ F_c := max{(a),(b)}`.

*Proof.* **(a)** any realizer of `w` realizes the prefix `P`. **(b)** `x_{u+1}`
realizes `Q`, so `x_{u+1} ≥ m(Q)`, and `m = (2^{S_{P'}}x_{u+1} − C_{P'})/3^{u+1}`
is increasing in `x_{u+1}`. **(c)** in `w = 1^u q_a 1^v q_b 1^t` the prefix `P`
is followed by `q_b ≥ 2`, so every realizer of `w` realizes `P` **as a closed
episode**; apply row two of Proposition 2. ∎

**Branch (c) is a strict improvement, and it is free.** It is available only
because the second defect closes the first episode — extra information the
two-defect structure supplies, not an alternative convention. Measured on
`u,v,t ∈ 1..11` (15552 configs): `F_c'` is **never worse** and strictly better
on **5442 (35.0%)**, mean gain **1.649 bits**, median 1.414, max 4.790;
truncation binds in 64.2%. The gain is `O(1)` bits and does **not** change the
exponent — the balanced median rises 0.6826 → 0.6903 against an empirical
1.0341 — so §5 and every asymptotic claim are unaffected.

**Framing [T3].** `F_c` is **not a new mechanism.** It is orbit-advance plus
truncation, evaluated at *one-defect granularity* — truncating to the full
one-defect prefix `P` and using its exact `m(P)`, rather than to a maximal
all-ones run. The novelty claimed here is only (a) closed-form
per-configuration evaluability and (b) the β-reduction of §5.

**Verification and independence certificate.** `F_c ≤ m(w)` with **0
violations / 19773**. Against the all-ones-run floor used by the first
implementation: better on **19252**, tied on **521**, worse on **0** —
dominant, strictly better on **97.4%**. The two independent implementations
agree: this one reports 0/19773 violations and 0 regressions; the chat-session
implementation reports 450/451 with one exact tie at a degenerate `|w|=32`,
`v=1` configuration. Same qualitative result on different grids.

## 5. The uniform reduction and the open import β

Theorem 5 is per-configuration. A *uniform* family floor needs a uniform
one-defect input. Import, as a named hypothesis:

> **(β)** `log₂ m(1^U q 1^V) ≥ β · S(1^U q 1^V)` for all admissible `U,q,V`.

Normalisation, stated explicitly: balanced `u=v=t=k`, `q_a=q_b=2`, so
`S_P = 2k+2`, and the comparator is the all-ones-run floor `k+1` bits.

- **Truncation branch:** `β(2k+2) > k+1 ⟺ β > (k+1)/(2k+2) = 1/2`, which is
  **exactly 1/2 for every `k`**, not merely asymptotically.
- **Advance branch:** `β > (α(k+1) − 1)/(2k+2) → α/2 = 0.792481`, rising
  `0.542 → 0.792` across `k = 1…5000`.

Empirical calibration: `β_emp` for `1^k 2 1^k` is 0.9167 (`k=5`), 0.9286 (20),
0.9672 (60), 1.0033 (150) — comfortably above both thresholds.

> **This is the single question to which the note reduces the two-defect
> problem:** is `(β)` provable with `β > 1/2`? It is **OPEN**, and it is an
> *effective* question — §8 records why.

**Deletion bridge — DOMINATED** by Theorem 5: it requires `β ≳ 0.56`, strictly
worse than the `1/2` of §5, and additionally needs a uniform-cost hypothesis
that `F_c` does not.

**Why no bridge can beat `F_c` [T3].** Condition (ii) of the bridge formalism,
`m(v) ≤ A·m(w)^q·2^{δ|w|}`, rearranges to
`m(w) ≥ (m(v)/(A·2^{δ|w|}))^{1/q}` — which *is* the bridge's conclusion. So
(ii) is never weaker than the floor it produces, and a bridge is non-circular
only when (ii) is established by a mechanism indifferent to the *size* of
`m(w)`. Exactly one such mechanism exists here: a realizer realizes every
contiguous **factor** of `w`, directly (truncation) or after orbit advance.
Those are the two branches of `F_c`. Merge and deletion images are not factors
of `w`, so they admit no non-circular (ii).

## 8. Non-claims and limitations

1. **Nothing here bears on the Collatz conjecture**, and nothing excludes
   divergent orbits. Every statement concerns least realizers of finite
   valuation words.
2. **No formal verification.** No Lean, no proof assistant. "Verified" means
   exhaustive or exact finite computation on a stated grid.
3. **Per-configuration ≠ uniform.** Theorem 5 is a certificate for each `w`
   separately. The uniform statement requires `(β)`, which is open.
4. **The residual above `F_c` is the incomplete-window statement.** Closing it
   requires that the canonical residue `−C_w 3^{−n} (mod 2^{S+1})` not be
   anomalously small. For two defects this is a **two-dimensional** pointwise
   window problem for `⟨3⟩ ≤ (Z/2^M)*` — simultaneous dyadic windows for
   `3^{−a}` and `3^{−a−b}` — whose one-dimensional case is the §2 one-defect
   problem. Character/complete-sum methods are provably unavailable there
   (the pointwise dangerous-window statement is false uniformly in the shift,
   2 being a primitive root mod `3^b`), and the two-dimensional case is
   strictly stronger, so that closure carries over a fortiori.
5. **Effective methods degrade with a second defect, categorically.** The
   two-defect realizer satisfies a three-logarithm 2-adic form with heights
   `(3, A, Z)`, `Z = 3^{v+1}(2^{q_a−1}−1) + 2^{q_a+v}(2^{q_b−1}−1)`. Here
   `log₂ Z = Θ(v)` exactly, and `Z` is **never** `{2,3}`-smooth (19773/19773),
   so the recoverable exponent fraction acquires a factor `1/v` and tends to 0
   for every fixed constant — except when the middle run is bounded, where the
   word is a one-defect word plus a bounded perturbation. The three-logarithm
   constant is **not verified at source**, so no crossover is asserted.
6. **The residual behaves as an independent residual (R6 upheld, on better
   grounds).** The published statistic — `(log₂m − F_c)/|w|` against P-anchor
   fullness `log₂m(P)/(u+q_a+v+1)`, random configurations at `|w|=122` —
   reproduces **exactly** in this environment: `r = −0.179`, `n = 100`. At that
   `n` the design is underpowered: across 40 replicates (seeds `20261023+i`)
   `r` ranges over `[−0.372, +0.042]`, sd 0.111, with `|r| > 0.197` firing in
   21/40, so neither the magnitude nor even the sign is stable. At `n = 20000`
   the same statistic gives `r = −0.1673`, which **is** nonzero (threshold
   0.0139) and agrees closely with the published point estimate. The
   correlation is therefore real but explains `r² = 2.8%` of residual variance.
   R6's conclusion stands; its stated justification should be **small effect
   size, not statistical insignificance**.
7. **Novelty is not assessed.** No literature search was performed for this
   note. Absence from a search would not be evidence of novelty in any case.
   Theorem 5's two inequalities are standard cylinder facts; the contribution
   claimed is the closed-form evaluation and the β-reduction.
